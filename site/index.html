The question

Accounts of hacking, collusion, and other harmful behaviour are increasingly available to AI models through web retrieval and may enter future training data.

We ask how evidence of past misconduct influences a model’s own decisions—particularly when the evidence describes an attempt that worked, failed, or was caught and penalised.

The experiment

Models solve difficult prime factorisation and subset sum tasks with prohibited shortcuts available. We vary the records of past attempts while keeping the task and available tools matched across conditions.

No past evidence

The baseline: a task and its rules, without a record of past cheating.

Ineffective

A past shortcut attempt failed to produce an accepted answer.

Effective, not caught

A past shortcut attempt succeeded and retained its credit.

Effective, caught

A past shortcut attempt succeeded, but was detected and penalised.

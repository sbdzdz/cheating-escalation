Public reports of AI security incidents describe hacking, collusion, and other harmful actions in detail. These reports are increasingly accessible to AI models through web retrieval and are likely to enter future training data. Could exposure to accounts of past misconduct encourage models to repeat, avoid, or escalate such behaviour?

We study this question in a simulated environment where models can use multiple prohibited shortcuts to solve difficult tasks. Models receive records of past cheating attempts that distinguish ineffective attempts, effective attempts that went undetected, and effective attempts that were detected and penalised.
